🎯 Objective
To apply knowledge in distinguishing and categorizing various types of computer components and hardware in real-world contexts (CLO1). This lab provides hands-on experience in the safe teardown and reassembly of a desktop computer system.

📝 Part 1: Hardware Teardown (Morning Session)
Guided by the lab instructor, our team successfully stripped down a desktop PC to its core motherboard architecture.
🔧 Step-by-Step Disassembly Protocol
We strictly followed the 7-step hardware safety and disassembly procedure:
1. Turn off plug: Ensured complete power disconnection to prevent electrical hazards.
2. Open casing: Removed the side panel to access the internal chassis.
3. Disconnect all cable: Safely unplugged SATA cables, ATX power connectors, and front-panel headers.
4. Take out power Supply (PSU): Unscrewed and removed the power delivery unit.
5. Take out the RAM: Released the retention clips and pulled out the memory modules.
6. Take out the hard disc drive (HDD): Unmounted the main storage drive from the bay.
7. Take out the CPU cooling fan: Unlatched the thermal cooling unit to expose the processor socket.

💡 Summary and Personal Reflection
This hands-on laboratory session effectively bridged the gap between the theoretical hardware concepts taught in Chapter 2 and real-world application.

Physically handling the motherboard and executing the 7-step disassembly process required precision and an understanding of hardware fragility (especially when handling the RAM and CPU cooler). Furthermore, collaborating in a 7-person team on a single PC during the BK10 session highlighted the importance of communication in IT environments. By the time we sat for the quiz in BK4, the abstract concepts of computer architecture had become tangible, practical knowledge.

This activity successfully met the course's goal of active, project-based learning and significantly improved my confidence in hardware engineering.
